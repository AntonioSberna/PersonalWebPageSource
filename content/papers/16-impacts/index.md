abstract: "Bridge piers and similar concrete-filled steel tube (CFST) columns can be struck by vehicles close to the base, where boundary restraint can strongly alter force transfer and damage localization. This study investigates the near-base lateral impact response of circular CFST bridge-pier columns without axial load. Eight one-third-scale specimens were tested using a horizontal rigid-hammer impact facility in cantilever and propped cantilever configurations, and four additional specimens reported previously were included to examine the effect of steel tube  thickness. Impact was applied at approximately one-quarter of the exposed column height, representing a typical vehicular-collision contact height. The work provides a direct and fully  instrumented experimental comparison of cantilever and propped cantilever boundary conditions for circular CFST bridge-pier-type columns under the same near-base lateral impact. Within the tested range, the boundary condition is a primary control on displacement demand, deformation pattern, energy absorption, and final damage mode. Cantilever specimens develop larger lateral  displacements and, at the higher velocities, absorb more energy through global flexural deformation, whereas propped cantilever specimens reduce the maximum displacement by 28--41% but have a longer primary contact, 9--17% higher impulse, and more localized damage near the impact region. The peak impact force increases mainly with impact velocity and is less sensitive to boundary condition, to the single impactor-mass increment, and to tube thickness. The thicker tube mainly reduces the displacement of the cantilever specimens. These findings refer to reduced-scale columns without axial load under rigid-hammer impact, not to full vehicle--pier collisions."

## Highlights

* Near-base impact tests on circular concrete-filled steel tube pier columns.
* Top restraint limits drift and moves damage from the base to the impact zone.
* Cantilever columns deform more and absorb more energy at the higher velocities.
* Propped-cantilever columns show longer contact and 9-17\% higher impulse.
* Thicker tubes reduced cantilever drift; peak force was nearly unchanged.
